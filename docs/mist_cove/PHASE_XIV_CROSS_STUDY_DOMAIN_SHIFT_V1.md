# Phase XIV: SC-to-ST domain shift

The original SC-trained model passes the minimal frozen ST transfer gate but loses substantial stage accuracy and calibration. Mean participant macro-F1 falls from 0.8160 on in-sample SC TRAIN to 0.5583 on ST; the cohorts are unpaired. REM recall falls from 0.7914 to 0.2162, N1 from 0.5044 to 0.2809 and N3 from 0.8690 to 0.7790. N2 recall changes less, 0.9207 to 0.9058. ECE increases from 0.0030 to 0.1339. A minimum transfer gate should not be described as uniform robustness.

Stage-balanced participant mean contrasts (ST minus SC) include EEG centered RMS **+20.73 µV**, bootstrap [17.21, 24.09]; absolute peak **+79.49 µV**, [65.69, 92.91]; sigma fraction **+0.00864**, [0.00361, 0.01413]. These are unpaired descriptive differences, not estimates of a single cause. Stage prevalence also differs strongly, notably prolonged SC Wake in the selected TRAIN material. ST nights differ in medication exposure, person selection, acquisition and possibly montage signal quality. Chin EMG and marker data have different native acquisition rates and cannot be pooled as equivalent measurements.

Automated spindle-like Fpz-Cz participant rates rise from 32.48 to 121.87 candidates per eligible hour; Pz-Oz from 36.38 to 113.99. The slow-wave-like Fpz-Cz rates are 145.30 versus 167.20, while Pz-Oz rates are 15.80 versus 54.11. Detector/montage behavior changes enough that a proxy is not a stable external biological reference without human verification. The same exact explanation operator transfers computationally, but compact active displays fall from 80/150 SC balanced cases to 98/219 ST cases, and mean clause-state coverage falls from 0.471 to 0.304. Different sampling rules, stage distributions and people prevent a paired causal interpretation of these descriptive differences.

The SC contrast is in-sample development; ST is frozen cross-study. The older approved VAL result is shown as a reused aggregate only, with no new VAL raw access. `cross_study_metrics.json` contains full participant-bootstrap feature contrasts and caveats. No independent clinical-population, SHHS, RESERVED, or prospective generalization claim follows.
