Two artificial tasks use five phase-coded, equal-DFT-magnitude templates (200 samples), generated from fixed Fourier magnitudes and seeded phases. They are synthetic class codes, not sleep events. Randomly placed diagnostic components have low amplitude; independent stronger nuisance templates, noise, slow background and amplitude/location jitter are independent of class. Substitutable training has one or two copies of one class code. Complementary training has adjacent codes on a five-code ring; either one alone is consistent with two labels. Symmetric single-factor removal avoids the Phase II v1 identification error.

Seeds 123/456/789, 300 generated TRAIN examples/task (60/class), 300 mini-batch-16 AdamW updates at lr 0.001, wd 0.0001, clip 5, deterministic FP32/two CPU threads. No held-out selection/early stopping or localization supervision. Each method gets identical generated examples and minibatch indices; final step is evaluated. Each task/seed trains M0, M1, M2, M3, M2_rms, M1_phase. Then M1/M2/M3 heads fit 150 updates on the exact same frozen final M1 encoder, shared cached features and examples. Encoder pretraining is common to that comparison; report this exposure separately. Fixed encoder comparisons cannot establish that the conventional pretraining objective is optimal for evidence.

Each of ten fixed conditions has 100 new examples/task/seed: clean, single, redundant, correlated, competitive, abrupt_artifact, overlap, partial, erased and eventless. Labels retained after complete erasure are used only to quantify irreducible loss/false confidence, not to pretend remaining information identifies the class. Nuisance amplitude and event settings are fixed in JSON. Training includes ordinary nuisance, while competitive/artifact conditions deliberately increase its strength/count. Spectrum-matched code swaps and tiny noise/gain diagnostics are controlled generated transformations, never assumed clinically label-preserving.
