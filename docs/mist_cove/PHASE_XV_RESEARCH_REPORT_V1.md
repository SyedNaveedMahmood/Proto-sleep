# MIST-COVE Phase XV: REM failure, proxy challenge, and operator reliability

**Research status:** `EXPLORATORY_REUSED_ST`. Sleep-Telemetry (ST) outcomes were already inspected in Phase XIV. The Phase XV lock is a reproducibility constraint on a retrospective investigation, not an untouched test or a new prospective success gate. The historical P3B `raw_context` model remained fixed. Phase XV made **zero neural fits, zero optimizer updates, and zero ST adaptations**. Human event validation remains **NOT TESTED**.

## Question and method

This phase asks why the authenticated single-lead classifier misses ST REM, and what exact response certificates add when a simple sigma-power interval selector better matches the automated Pz-Oz spindle-like proxy. The comparison separates four different things: the scored 30-second sleep stage, the model's response to feature withholding, the automated candidate event, and the effect of physically replacing waveform samples. None is interchangeable with the others.

The source classifier receives Fpz-Cz EEG only. The retrospective diagnostic read covered the same 44 authorized ST nights from 22 people and the approved Fpz-Cz, Pz-Oz, horizontal EOG, and native chin EMG channels. The complete classification grid and stage-blind event outputs came from verified Phase XIV caches. One new read-only ST pass produced calibrated waveform diagnostics for all 8,349 scored REM epochs and 360 private crops for the sealed explanation panels. There were no new SC VAL, SC RESERVED, SHHS, other-cohort, or external EEG reads. SC TRAIN prior results were inherited, not retrained.

The Phase XV plan was committed before case selection. E1 selects at most one scored epoch per stage, person, and night by a fixed hash independent of predictions and detectors (219 cases). E2 is its fixed Pz-Oz spindle-candidate subset (63). E3 contains 40 each of REM→W, REM→N1, REM→N2, correct REM, correct N2, and non-REM error (240). The union has 360 distinct cases; E1 and E3 overlap in 99. E3 estimates behavior in selected failure strata, not error prevalence. The private coordinate list is sealed by a published digest; crops have a separate byte digest and ordered-coordinate digest. No coordinate list or waveform was committed.

For each union case, the source operator was checked against the original native full-input logits. We evaluated both registered neutral states on every one of the 32,768 availability masks. Every Boolean response table was compiled to a fixed-order reduced decision diagram and its displayed clauses were checked against all rows. The target was the original predicted class, including wrong predictions. Equal-budget spectral, RMS, gradient, random, and position selectors were matched to the exact-positive mask's count of nonoverlapping two-second physical atoms. The greedy sufficient subset retained its own length. Proxy precision was computed only after method selection, using stage-blind event coordinates. Intervals, empty or whole-epoch support, and absence of a detector candidate are reported separately.

## REM failure on the complete ST grid

The original 42,691 scored-epoch confusion matrix was reproduced exactly. Among **8,349 REM epochs**, the model predicted W for **2,322**, N1 for **1,570**, N2 for **2,648**, N3 for **4**, and REM for **1,805**: REM recall **0.2162**. Every one of the 22 people had scored REM and each of the three common error types occurred in all 22. Person-level REM recall averaged **0.2211**. The four REM→N3 epochs came from two people and are too few for a stable subtype inference.

The error groups differ rather than fitting one simple failure story. Relative to correct REM, REM→N2 had higher frontal sigma fraction (person-average difference **+0.018**, 95% person-bootstrap interval **[+0.013,+0.023]**) and was more often within two scored epochs of a transition (**+0.165**, **[+0.113,+0.224]**). Its automated EOG excursion count was lower (**−3.57** per epoch, **[−4.45,−2.74]**). REM→W had *more* automated EOG excursions (**+5.61**, **[+4.54,+6.75]**) and lower frontal sigma fraction (**−0.006**, **[−0.008,−0.004]**). In this historical Fpz-Cz-only classifier, conspicuous EOG activity can coexist with a wrong Wake response; it does not by itself adjudicate the scorer or establish a missing-channel cause. REM→N1 had much smaller model margin and higher predictive entropy than the other common error groups. These are exploratory associations, with multiple related comparisons and no causal adjustment.

The diagnostic signals retain their acquisition meanings. Both EEG leads and horizontal EOG are calibrated at 100 Hz. ST chin EMG is native 100 Hz, including a within-night RMS rank and 20–45 Hz measure; it was never pooled as equivalent to the processed 1 Hz SC chin envelope. The full-grid fixed quality flags show, for example, Fpz-Cz step candidates in 3.1% of REM→W versus 1.5% of correct REM epochs, and Pz-Oz extreme-amplitude candidates in 2.7% versus 0.6%. These flags are detector outputs, not artifact or clinical diagnoses. No transition measure crosses an unscored physical position or a recording boundary; an early analysis version that could span such a gap was corrected before final inference and tested.

Medication condition is confounded with the original paired study design and recording circumstances. Across 22 within-person pairs, the mean temazepam-minus-placebo REM recall difference was **+0.0124** (95% person-bootstrap interval **[−0.0323,+0.0563]**). This analysis does not estimate a causal drug effect. The original ST pooled 15-bin ECE was reproduced at **0.133863**, with **3,209 high-confidence wrong** scored epochs. Stage-specific ECE was **0.453 for REM** and **0.425 for N1**. Among scored REM, fixed `margin < 0.5` flagged 27.0% of wrong cases but falsely flagged 35.7% of correct cases. Fixed `entropy > 1.1` flagged 27.7% of wrong cases and 32.1% of correct cases. A high-confidence response was itself wrong in 94.4% of the 1,830 REM epochs where predicted confidence reached 0.9. None of these rules was fitted to ST or is a deployable safety screen.

## Exact circuits and the physiological-proxy challenge

All **360 new base tables and 360 neutral-state tables** completed. The maximum full-input logit discrepancy was **0.0** in the audited float32 path. The authenticated model's named state was unchanged. On E1, **187/219** response functions had an edge where adding availability removed the target response, **92/219** had a compact active signed clause under the fixed display rule, **19/219** had a prior response, **66/219** needed whole-epoch positive support, and **190/219** changed at least one response-table entry when neutral state changed. The 32,768-state guarantee is an internal model claim under the specified withholding operator. It does not mean the selected EEG interval is a verified spindle or that the classifier is correct.

E1 had 63 epochs with a Pz-Oz spindle-like candidate; eight had whole-epoch positive support. This left **55 eligible cases from 20 people** for matched interval precision. Mean precision was **0.171** for exact-positive support and **0.217** for sigma-ranked atoms. The person-mean exact-minus-sigma difference was **−0.0462**, with 1,000-person-bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.0797,−0.0167]**. Sigma won 24 cases, exact won 9, and 22 tied. Exact exceeded matched RMS (**+0.1072** person mean), random (**+0.1379**), delta (**+0.1470**), and gradient (**+0.1145**), but this does not rescue the specific sigma comparison. The previously registered Phase XIV sigma-increment gate remains **FAIL**, and the earlier SC developmental proxy gate remains **NOT ESTIMABLE**. The new ST comparison is exploratory; its interval does not create a fresh confirmatory result.

E3 supplied 50 eligible proxy cases; mean exact and sigma precision were **0.191** and **0.244**. Of E2's 55 eligible cases, 18 had an incorrect stage prediction. Their exact-minus-sigma mean remained negative (**−0.0345** at the case level). A wrong prediction can still have a mathematically faithful response certificate. The 10-second proxy-shift and next-physical-epoch Pz-Oz controls reduced mean exact-support precision to **0.0586** and **0.0860**, respectively. These controls make timing nontrivial, but the target remains an unvalidated automatic detector, not an expert event label. Alternative Pz-Oz slow-wave, frontal spindle, EOG excursion, and EMG low-tone candidate comparisons are reported with their own denominators; they cannot be interpreted as interchangeable clinical endpoints.

The exact 15-atom table costs 32,768 cached-head states per neutral state, plus feature-cache and native forward work. A spectral selector is much cheaper. The Phase XV run charged **23,594,760** attempted-inclusive head states for base and neutral tables, **9,830,700** for three nuisance views on 100 E1 cases, and **1,080** for branch ablations: **33,426,540** total of the **38,000,000** limit. This accounting does not mislabel cached-head states as full-network training exposures. The 100 E1 nuisance cases were taken in already sealed hash order, without outcome or proxy reselection.

Prediction stability and certificate stability diverged. Under mild high-frequency nuisance, **93/100** predictions stayed the same while only **70/100** original primary clauses stayed valid; **3** retained a different original active clause after the primary failed. Drift yielded **98/100** stable predictions, **78/100** stable primary clauses, and **1** substitution. Gain yielded **96/100** for both prediction and primary clause stability. These are fixed-model view changes, not physiology. The branch ablation found 36/219 E1 cases where the global-only branch retained the original prediction while local-only changed it; seven of those were wrong predictions. The branch result indicates model reliance under an internal decomposition, not that a physical channel caused the error.

The inherited Phase XIV ST physical-replacement audit remains the relevant signal-domain comparison: bridge replacements were compatible in **500/3,285** requests, with **102/500** structural-versus-physical disagreements; background replacements were compatible in **94/3,285** requests, with **7/94** disagreements. Many physical replacements fail compatibility or eligibility. A successful internal withholding certificate therefore cannot be read as a valid physical deletion, and neither operator establishes preservation or removal of a clinical event. We did not weaken historical donor guards.

## Integrity, resource use, and limits

The starting tree, committed protocol, Phase XIV public manifest (103 files), original source checkpoint byte digest, named state digest, 29 approved SC TRAIN and 44 ST record identities, and the Phase XIV detector/circuit cache receipts were checked. The original source model was restricted-loaded only through its established authenticated path. Phase XIV's WP23 ledger still says `PENDING_AGGREGATION` despite completed SC witness execution; the Phase XV corrigendum records that bookkeeping issue and leaves the old files and scientific gate unchanged.

The new ST raw-value pass opened the 44 authorized ST PSGs and no other cohort. Its recorded wall time was **21.65 s**; exhaustive explanation execution was **229.90 s**; nuisance tables **87.4 s**; branch audit **1.36 s**. Other aggregation/test stages are accounted separately in the resource receipt. The full CPU suite passed with 11 CUDA-only skips; no GPU or CUDA operation was used for scientific work. No private waveform, per-epoch prediction array, source path, subject annotation, fitted weight, or normalization value is included in this report or the public figures. The ten-case calibrated waveform atlas remains in the ignored private workspace. No qualified blinded reviewers were available, so its human validation status is **NOT TESTED**.

The first 20 exact cases were monitored as an in-run throughput and identity check; a separately halted resource pilot was not executed. This process deviation did not change the frozen panel, model, methods, or query ceiling and is recorded here rather than silently described as a prospective pilot. The 100-case nuisance subset and the Phase XV analyses are exploratory after prior ST exposure. Automated candidate events can disagree with expert annotations, and a precision gain against a proxy would not establish clinical event localization. Small proxy-eligible denominators and nested nights constrain inference. The REM signal contrasts are observational; model branch and mask interventions do not identify causal physiology. No Phase XVI study or new neural training was initiated.

The narrowest defensible contribution is an exact, dependency-aware audit of this frozen EEG classifier's response under a declared missing-information operator, with explicit demonstrations of nonmonotonicity, alternative witnesses, neutral sensitivity, and differences from physical signal changes. The current evidence does **not** support claiming superior spindle-like localization over a simple sigma baseline or robust REM staging across this source-to-ST shift. The highest-value next independent study is a blinded, qualified event-annotation comparison on a genuinely new validation boundary, coupled to a prespecified sigma baseline; classifier improvement should be a separately authorized development program. See the Phase XVI proposal for design only.
